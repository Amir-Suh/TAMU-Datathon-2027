# Adversarial review of `gemini_output/*`

Reviewed 2026-11-07 against the kit in `/home/ganfwe/Downloads/main-participants-60-40`. Every verdict below is from a command run in this session (scripts in the session scratchpad; the reproducing commands are in the last section). Anything not run is marked UNVERIFIED.

## Verdict in one paragraph
Gemini's documents are a good glossary and a reasonable menu of model families, and the kit-baseline numbers it quotes are exactly right. The strategy built on top of them has four faults that would cost real points: (1) it describes the problem as "90% aleatoric noise" when the validation targets show the opposite, (2) it asserts lag rules that the data does not show, (3) it counts 140k history rows as 140k samples when the regime phenomena have only 96 training episodes, and (4) it forbids using validation and test data in the ways that matter most while ignoring the biggest free data source, the test histories. It also misses two hard constraints (`signal` exists only at the cutoff; round two changes the lag structure) and the 1e-8 reproduction risk.

## Things Gemini got right (keep)
- The glossary and ID hierarchy (01 sections 1–4) are accurate; `machine_type` means of true `p_0` match its numbers (measured steady 0.403, balanced 0.526, jackpot 0.754).
- Family list: Bayesian shrinkage, GBDT with lag/EMA/neighbour features, HMM/regime, small sequence model, blend. The bake-off below keeps all five, with changes.
- Inference cost is negligible for 1,536 queries; train on GPU, ship numpy.

## UNVERIFIED (not run)
- Gemini's per-signal jackpot rates (`p_0` 0.828 / 0.757 / 0.688) and the 56.2 / 19.9 / 15.3 / 7.1 / 1.5 overall payout frequencies.
- Whether any specific lag (1–8) is individually predictive; that is what the GLM pilot measures.
